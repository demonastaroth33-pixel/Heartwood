## 1. THE STAGE MODEL (seed → seedling → sapling → pole → mature → old-growth)

**Botanical answer to the framing question:** a real dicot tree's first *branches*
appear from axillary buds that exist from the first true leaves (Part 4.3), but
apical dominance suppresses them through the sapling phase (Part 3.4, Part 4.5);
a first-year oak is "one 20 cm shoot" (Part 3.3). First *flowers* come at
maturity — oak ~20–40 years, apple ~5–8 (Part 10.7, Part 11.2) — and the master
states outright that "maturity is measured in size/mass more than age (bonsai
stay juvenile long; a grafted mature scion flowers young)" (Part 10.7). First
*fruits* follow flowers by one season (Part 10.7: "the flower-to-fruit pipeline
takes one season for most temperate trees — apple: May flowers → October
apples"). So the design's compression is defensible ONLY if (a) it is explicitly
framed as a time-lapse compression and (b) it uses the master's own levers:
size/mass maturity (Part 10.7) and pioneer-species speed (Part 11.3). The docs
currently assert botanical literalness ("botany + gamification both satisfied",
LOOPHOLES L-02) without naming the compression — that is where the errors creep in.

### C-1 — CRITICAL — First bloom at SAPLING contradicts the authority's own sapling definition
- **Location:** LOOPHOLES.md §2 SAPLING row ("FIRST BLOOM (the banked buds burst)"),
  §1 ("at the flowering stage (first qualifying year)…"), stage×class matrix
  class-6 SAPLING cell; VISION principle 14b.
- **What the botany says:** Part 11.2 stage 3 defines the sapling as "all
  investment — **no flowers yet**"; flowering begins at the MATURE stage
  (Part 11.2 stage 5). The master's own Life Tree bridge (Part 13.1) maps first
  flowers to "POLE → MATURE: … first flowers = first achievements". Putting
  FIRST BLOOM at SAPLING (~year 1–2) is therefore a contradiction of the
  document the design itself declares as its authority — and it is a ~3–20×
  compression vs the fastest real trees (apple 5–8, Part 10.7).
- **Correction:** move FIRST BLOOM to the POLE stage (year 2–4). SAPLING keeps
  bud-swelling + bud-burst into *leaves* only (the D087 habit-bud system and the
  achievement buds swell; they do not bloom). POLE = "first bloom; flowers bloom
  directly thereafter"; MATURE = "full flowering + rare tiers". This matches
  Part 13.1 exactly. If the user insists on keeping the bloom at sapling, it
  must be re-framed, not asserted: (1) explicit compression statement in the
  why-panel ("your tree flowered at the pace of a fast-growing pioneer on ideal
  soil" — Part 11.3), and (2) the size/mass lever (Part 10.7: the tree reached
  reproductive mass, not age). Never state "botany satisfied" without one of
  these.

### C-2 — CRITICAL — The SEED stage displays "5 branch-buds on the trunk"; a seed has no trunk
- **Location:** LOOPHOLES.md §2 SEED row ("0 (5 branch-buds on the trunk)"),
  §1 banking-forms ("sections present from day one as buds on the trunk"),
  L-03 resolution ("the seedling's architecture is set, growth varies — real
  botany").
- **What the botany says:** Part 1.2 — a dicot seed is coat + embryo (radicle,
  plumule, hypocotyl, epicotyl, 2 cotyledons) + food. There is no trunk, no
  stem, no buds. The entire future shoot exists as the plumule — "the embryonic
  shoot (first stem + first true leaves in bud form)" — inside the coat.
- **Correction:** the SEED visual is a closed package; the plumule's latent
  shoot is visible only in an anatomy/cross-section view (which the design's
  principle 16 already provides). The 5 branch-buds appear on the stem at the
  SEEDLING stage — which is exactly where the L-03 claim is botanically true:
  axillary buds exist from the first true leaves (Part 4.3), apical dominance
  holds them in check (Part 4.5). "Architecture set, growth varies" is a
  SEEDLING fact, not a SEED fact.

### M-1 — MAJOR — First branch extends at SEEDLING (weeks–months); real seedlings are single-leader shoots
- **Location:** LOOPHOLES.md §2 SEEDLING row ("first branch extends on earned
  presence").
- **What the botany says:** Part 3.3 — "the first year of an oak might be one
  20 cm shoot with a deep root"; Part 3.4 — the sapling transition is defined by
  "strong apical dominance… a straight single trunk". A weeks-old dicot seedling
  does not extend branches; it establishes roots first ("roots first, then
  shoots", Part 3.3).
- **Correction:** SEEDLING = branch-BUDS + leaves only; the first branch
  extension is the SAPLING milestone (year 1–2) — which the design already
  calls "a visible milestone" (L-03). Move it one row down. (Defensible-but-weak
  as-is: shade-grown/damaged seedlings do occasionally branch early, Part 4.5.)

### M-2 — MAJOR — The timeline compression is never stated; "mature at 4+", "old-growth at decade+" are asserted as literal
- **Location:** LOOPHOLES.md §2 (all stage years), VISION principle 2.
- **What the botany says:** Part 10.7 (maturity 5–40 yrs), Part 11.4 (dicot
  lifespans 60–2,300 yrs — old-growth is measured in centuries, not decades),
  Part 11.2 ("a tree's age is not its size"). The design's spine runs ~10×
  faster than reality.
- **Correction:** add one locked sentence to LOOPHOLES §2 or VISION 14b: "The
  stage timeline is a time-lapse compression of the botanical spine (Part 11.2);
  stage boundaries follow botanical form, not literal years; maturity gates use
  the size/mass lever (Part 10.7) and pioneer speed (Part 11.3)." The why-panel
  states it once per user. This single framing upgrade converts C-1's residue
  and the old-growth gap (m-1) from contradictions into honest design.

### M-10 — MAJOR — First fruits at SAPLING are only defensible because bloom also sits at SAPLING
- **Location:** LOOPHOLES.md stage×class matrix class-5 SAPLING cell ("fruit
  buds → first fruits").
- **What the botany says:** a fruit is the mature ovary of a flower (Part 10.5);
  fruit presupposes bloom, one season earlier (Part 10.7: May flowers →
  October apples). The row is legal only while the bloom is in the same stage.
- **Correction:** move "first fruits" to POLE together with C-1's bloom move
  (bloom in the POLE spring → fruits on spurs in the POLE autumn — one season,
  Part 10.7). Also relabel SEEDLING's "fruit buds" as flower buds: a fruit bud
  IS a flower bud (Part 4.5 fruit spurs — spurs "carry the flowers and fruit").

## 2. THE BRANCH-BUD CONCEPT (sections as branch-buds on the trunk from day one)

**Botanical answer:** sound at the seedling; wrong at the seed (C-2). The bud is
the correct latent-shoot object: an axillary bud "can grow into a branch,
flower, or thorn" (Part 4.3); branching is the interplay of apical dominance and
light (Part 4.5); monopodial trees keep one leader with ordered side branches
(Part 4.5, Part 11.2 stage 3). The design's "leader = most sustained domain"
maps cleanly onto monopodial/excurrent architecture, and the D088 "fixed
branches, effort rhythms vary" user correction is exactly the botanical truth
that architecture is set while growth varies. No finding beyond C-2/M-1 — but
the docs should say the 5 section-buds are a tracked abstraction over the
seedling's many axillary buds (a real seedling carries buds at every leaf axil,
Part 4.3), so the count "5" reads as the engine's focus, not the plant's plan.

## 3. THE LEAF-CLUSTER CONCEPT (early aggregation of entries)

**Botanical answer:** the aggregation concept is coherent, and real botany names
it exactly — **a compound leaf** (Part 6.3): one leaf, many leaflets on a rachis,
"the whole leaf falls as one unit". The design's "a twig's worth of entries =
one leaf cluster" IS a pinnately compound leaf; its autumn fate matches the
master's own review metaphor (Part 13.7 leaf-fall = the yearly review).

**Real seedling leaf counts (the scaling answer):** cotyledons (2) then true
leaves as primordia on the shoot apex (Part 6.4); leaf number scales with shoot
extension — first-year oak = one 20 cm shoot with a handful of leaves (Part 3.3),
a few dozen by year 2–3, hundreds by pole, thousands by mature canopy. The
design's "a handful of leaves, never 50" at seedling (L-04) is the right order
of magnitude.

### M-3 — MAJOR — "Leaf cluster" should be named what botany calls it: a compound leaf, with heteroblastic progression
- **Location:** LOOPHOLES.md §1/§3 (leaf clusters), L-04.
- **What the botany says:** Part 6.3 (compound leaf = leaflets on a rachis,
  falls as one unit), Part 3.2 (heteroblasty: juvenile vs adult morphology —
  eucalyptus, acacia, ivy; "the engine's visual stages should have their own
  morphology, not just size scaling").
- **Correction:** rename "leaf clusters" → "compound leaves (leaflets =
  entries)" in the contract; the per-entry granularity unlock at POLE becomes a
  heteroblastic progression (juvenile compound/aggregate → adult granular
  leaves), which is textbook botany (Part 3.2) and gives the stages distinct
  morphology for free. This also makes the autumn "cluster falls as one unit"
  (Part 6.3) a literal truth.

### m-4 — MINOR — Per-entry granularity at POLE can still overflow a heavy journaler
- **Location:** LOOPHOLES.md §2 POLE row ("per-entry granularity begins").
- **What the botany says:** a 2–4-year-old tree carries hundreds, not thousands,
  of leaves (Part 3.3 scaling); a user with 2,000 entries at year 3 would
  overflow a real pole's canopy.
- **Correction:** keep cluster-banking as the overflow at EVERY stage with
  explicit per-stage leaf caps (the engine contract, Step 8); only MATURE's
  full-granularity mode is safe at thousands of leaves (a mature canopy holds
  tens of thousands honestly).

## 4. THE ACHIEVEMENT-BUD → FIRST BLOOM

**Botanical answer:** the framing is RIGHT — this is the most botanically
accurate part of the model. A bud that "will become a flower" is exactly what
real botany calls a flower bud (Part 4.3: an axillary bud "can grow into a
branch, flower, or thorn"); real flowers are "built last year's buds, using
stored reserves" and burst at spring budburst (Part 9.5 spring; Part 9.1 BBCH
budburst stages). Achievement-buds = flower buds formed during juvenility;
first bloom = the tree's commitment to reproduction at maturity (Part 10.7,
Part 11.2 stage 5). The only defects are placement (C-1) and ceremony wording
(m-2).

### m-2 — MINOR — "All banked buds burst into the first bloom together" is a spring-flush event, not a first-flowering literal
- **Location:** LOOPHOLES.md §1 first-flowering event; VISION 14b.
- **What the botany says:** real first flowerings are sparse and staggered
  (Part 10.7; BBCH 60–69 spans the season, Part 9.1), but synchronized mass
  burst IS real for temperate trees — the spring flush (Part 9.4: "some trees
  flush ALL leaves at once — spring burst — most temperate trees") and
  bloom-before-leaf (Part 9.5 spring: cherry, willow, hazel).
- **Correction:** frame the ceremony as the tree's first spring FLUSH — the
  cherry-blossom moment is botanically the flush, not the first flower. No
  behavior change; one sentence in the why-panel.

## 6. THE ADAPTATIONS AT THEIR STAGES (D088 + D089)

**Overall:** the D089 tenure floor (2+ qualifying years for structural
modifications) correctly keeps all silhouette-level adaptations off the
seed/seedling — real botany never shows a caudex or buttress on a seedling
(Part 4.6.10, Part 5.6.5: these are decades-scale, mature-tree structures even
in real time; under the compressed timeline, 2+ years is the honest floor).
The thorn question the task raises — "a seedling with thorns?" — is
botanically fine even at young ages: juvenile thorniness is real (honey locust
and citrus saplings are heavily thorned — Part 4.6.8), and the D089 floor puts
thorns at 365-day/2+ years anyway. The subtle tier's no-gate members are mostly
age-appropriate (reaction wood in a leaning year-1 stem, Part 7.4; epicormic
after any damage, Part 4.5; mycorrhizae from germination, Part 5.4; bud
scales, Part 6.5.11; spines at 100 days, Part 6.5.2). Two rows are wrong.

### C-3 — CRITICAL — "Stolons" for cross-domain influence is a mislabel that contradicts the design's own honest-skip logic
- **Location:** D088 adaptation row 11 (TEMP-PLANNING tree-7; 07-ledger
  §C row 11): "STOLONS — sustained cross-domain influences: the influencing
  branch grows toward the influenced one — MASTER 4.6.2".
- **What the botany says:** Part 4.6.2 — a stolon is a horizontal
  ABOVE-GROUND runner that roots at its nodes and makes NEW PLANTLETS
  (strawberry, spider plant). A branch growing toward another branch of the
  same tree is not a stolon — real branches do not root into their own trunk.
  Worse: the design's own HONEST SKIPS list excludes rhizomes/bulbils/offsets
  because "clonal spread needs a second tree — there is only the user's" —
  the exact same reason that should have excluded stolons. The row is
  internally contradictory.
- **Correction:** rename the visual (e.g., "reaching growth" — the influencing
  branch extends toward the influenced branch's light-space; canopy
  interdigitation is real, Part 4.5 crown architecture) OR re-anchor to the
  genuinely tree-real structure for cross-organism influence: the mycorrhizal
  wood-wide-web (Part 5.4) or intraspecific root grafting (Part 5.7). If kept
  as a stolon, it must be shown at ground level making a NEW plantlet — which
  the design has explicitly ruled out.

### M-6 — MAJOR — Contractile roots are an herbaceous/bulb mechanism, not a tree mechanism
- **Location:** D088 adaptation row 9 (TEMP-PLANNING tree-7): "CONTRACTILE

- **What the botany says:** Part 5.6.9 — contractile roots "pull the plant DOWN

- **Correction:** replace with the real tree mechanism for "plants itself
  deeper": taproot deepening (Part 12.2 — "roots grow deeper (the taproot's
  payoff)"; Part 5.6.1 storage taproot). Merge into row 8 (storage taproot +
  winter storage) as the deepening detail, or rename the row "deepening
  taproot".

### M-9 — MAJOR — The matrix gates ALL adaptations to MATURE+, contradicting D089's no-gate subtle tier
- **Location:** LOOPHOLES.md stage×class matrix class-7 MATURE cell ("full
  dormancy + adaptations") vs D089 (TEMP-PLANNING tree-7): subtle tier "no

- **What the botany says:** the subtle members are age-honest from year 1–2
  (reaction wood Part 7.4; epicormic Part 4.5; spines Part 6.5.2), so gating

## 7. SEASONS VS YOUNG TREES (the open hot zone)

**Botanical answer to "how should the stage model handle it":** three facts
resolve the hot zone without a new system. (1) A SEED has no phenology — it is
a dormant package whose only state is dormancy-until-trigger (Part 1.4); the
SEED stage should be season-invariant by definition, which the design already
implies. (2) Real first-year seedlings DO experience autumn: they abscise
their leaves (Part 9.5) and overwinter as a bare stem with scaled buds
(Part 9.2, Part 6.5.11) — "nothing to shed" is true only for a 0-leaf seedling
(e.g., germinated late), and even then the honest visual is a bare young stem,
not an evergreen. (3) Deciduousness is one of four real leaf economies
(Part 9.4), and the design's own axis system can drive it.

### M-8 — MAJOR — Young trees should keep the full season cycle at capacity scale; leaf retention should be character-driven, not universally deciduous
- **Location:** LOOPHOLES.md §7 open hot zone ("Seasonal cycle vs a newborn
  tree"); stage×class matrix class-7 rows (dormancy gated to SAPLING+).
- **What the botany says:** Part 9.5 (abscission is a year-1 reality),
  Part 9.2 (dormancy is a year-1 reality — "the tree sits as a closed,
  breathing-less skeleton"; even a first-year seedling is a bare stem +
  scaled buds), Part 9.4 (deciduous / evergreen / drought-deciduous /
  marcescent).
- **Correction:** (a) SEED = season-invariant (locked as such); (b) SEEDLING+
  = full season cycle with capacity-limited expression — a seedling autumn
  drops its handful of leaves honestly; a seedling winter is a tiny bare
  skeleton (that bare-silhouette winter view, Part 9.5, is the honest reveal of
  the branch-buds — a lovely first-winter moment); (c) leaf retention is a
  character trait from the axes (resource/rhythm position → evergreen,
  deciduous, drought-deciduous, or marcescent, Part 9.4) — marcescence
  (Part 9.4, beech holding dead leaves through winter) is a beautiful option
  for the "reluctant winter logger" the D085 intensity model already imagines;
  (d) move dormancy expression down: SAPLING = first dormancy, SEEDLING = the
  season cycle begins at capacity scale.

### m-1 — MINOR — OLD-GROWTH "the largest visuals" vs real old-growth's reduced reproduction; add the narrow-ring honesty
- **Location:** LOOPHOLES.md §2 OLD-GROWTH row; stage×class matrix class-6
  OLD-GROWTH cell.
- **What the botany says:** Part 11.2 stage 6 — old-growth = crown dieback,
  minimal growth, reduced fruit; Part 11.6 — "old trees: narrow rings". The
  design gives old-growth the LARGEST visuals.
- **Correction:** keep the reward (it is the game's top tier) but add the
  honesty layer: old-growth rings render narrow and dense (Part 11.6); the
